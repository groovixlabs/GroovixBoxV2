Note mode R3

Program the selected track. The top four rows are its 32 steps; the bottom four rows are a chromatic keyboard.

Steps

  • Tap an empty step to switch it on. Press or hold a lit step to see its notes: it stays on. Tapping never switches a step off — to remove one, hold R5 and tap it, as on Novation’s Circuit Tracks.
  • A newly switched-on step plays the last note you assigned on that track. At first that is the track’s default drum note.
  • Hold note keys and press a step to give that step the chord you are holding, up to 4 notes. Or hold steps and tap note keys: a key adds its note to the step, or takes it out if the step already has it. Taking out its last note empties the step.
  • Holding a step lights every note of its chord on the keyboard in green. While playing, the notes of the step under the playhead light in the track colour.
  • R5 + step clears it. R6 + step + step copies a step within the track.
  • Hold R3 and press B1–B8 to show another page of 32 steps. While R3 is held the track buttons show the pages, and the page the playhead is on blinks.
  • SHIFT + a step makes it the pattern's last step. It lights red and the steps after it go dark. Patterns run up to 8 pages, 256 steps.

Keyboard

  • The keyboard holds the notes of the song’s scale, or the track’s own scale, starting on its root in the track's octave (2 by default) at bottom-left and rising one scale note per pad, left to right and row by row upwards. Every row starts on the root, so a 7-note scale fills a row with one octave: the octave root sits on the eighth pad and again at the start of the row above. Chromatic keeps 8 semitones per row instead, which puts its roots on a diagonal, and runs from C2 (MIDI 36) to G4 (MIDI 67).
  • A track can have its own scale. Turn it on with the bottom-right pad in Scale mode, then pick the track’s root and scale there; the other tracks stay in the song’s key. For every note from C, pick Chromatic with root C.
  • Drum tracks show drum pads. Turn it on with the second-to-last pad of the bottom row in Scale mode. The keyboard becomes two 4×4 blocks, like a drum machine’s pads. Each block plays consecutive notes, rising left to right and then bottom to top: the left block starts on C in the track’s octave (C2–D#3 by default, kick at bottom-left) and the right block carries on (E3–G4). The left block is brighter so the two read apart. Shift + the leftmost or rightmost pad still moves both blocks an octave.
  • Root notes are tinted in the track colour so you can find the octaves. Pads past MIDI note 127 stay dark.
  • SHIFT + the leftmost or rightmost key moves the whole keyboard down or up an octave, and plays no note. Each track keeps its own octave, so a bass track can sit low and a lead high, and it is saved with the project.
  • A MIDI keyboard plays the selected track. Plug one in and its notes sound on that track's channel and port, at the velocity you played — and with R7 Record on and playback running they are written at the playhead, exactly as the pads are. It follows the track, not the mode, so you can play while looking at the patterns or the song. A key you hold lights the pad that plays the same note, so what your hands are doing shows on the grid; rows repeat the root, so one note can light two pads. Its Start and Stop keys drive the sequencer too, so your hands needn't leave it. The keyboard is found at startup, and again from the devices page; name one in the config with midiin when there is more than one thing to listen to.
  • One key, one chord. Turn on TRIAD or 7TH in Scale mode and every key plays three or four notes instead of one: its own note, and the rest of the scale stacked in thirds above it. The chord is built from the scale, so it is always in key — in a minor scale the first key gives a minor chord and the fifth a major one, with nothing to set up. It works everywhere a key does: press one to hear the chord, hold a step and press a key to write the chord onto it (pressing the same key again lifts it out), or play it in while recording. A seventh is four notes, which is exactly what a step holds, so a second key held with it adds only what fits. SHIFT + a key plays that one note, for a line inside a chord track, and the keys carry a hint of the track colour so you can see the track is in chords before you play one. Chords, Drums and the piano roll are three ways to use the same grid, so turning one on turns the others off: a drum track has no scale to build a chord from, and the roll has no keyboard to play one on. A track’s own scale does go with chords — they are built from the scale the track uses.
  • Pressing a note pad plays that note for as long as you hold it.
  • With R7 Record on and playback running, the note is also written into the step nearest to when you pressed it. In the second half of a step it goes to the next step.
  • The grid says when it is listening. The playhead runs white normally and red while R7 Record is on, so you can see that what you play is being written, and which step it will land on, without looking away from your hands. Red is never a track colour, so there is nothing for it to be confused with.
  • How hard you hit the pad is kept. The APC's pads are velocity sensitive, so a part played in arrives with its accents: the hit sets the step's velocity, which you can see and change afterwards in Step parameters. Velocity belongs to the step rather than to each note, so the last hit into a step speaks for its whole chord. The simulator's mouse cannot tell how hard, so a step written with it keeps the velocity it had.

Piano roll

A track can show its notes as a piano roll instead: turn it on with the first pad of the bottom row in Scale mode, on its own at the left end. The whole grid then edits one stretch of the pattern. Columns are 8 steps; rows are 8 notes of the track’s scale, lowest at the bottom, starting on the root in the track’s octave.

  • Tap a pad to add that note to the step; tap it again to take it out. Hold a pad to hear its note without changing the step. A step holds up to 4 notes and switches off when its last note goes.
  • SHIFT + B5 / B6 scroll the notes up or down, and SHIFT + B7 / B8 the steps left or right, 4 at a time so half of what you saw stays on screen. While SHIFT is held those buttons light where there’s further to go, and B1–B4 still change the track page.
  • SHIFT + a pad on the bottom row makes its step the pattern’s last. Steps past the end are dark.
  • Hold R3 and press B1–B8 to jump to a step page. R5 + pad clears a step and R6 + pad + pad copies one.
  • The rows follow the track’s keyboard: the song’s scale, its own scale, or every note from C on a drum track. Each track remembers where you scrolled.

Tracks

B1–B8 select tracks on the current track page. SHIFT + B1–B4 changes the track page (4 pages, tracks 1–32). See Pages.
