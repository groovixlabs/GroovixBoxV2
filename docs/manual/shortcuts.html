Keyboard shortcuts

Every button on the device has a key in the simulator, and the mouse presses pads. Keys and clicks combine: hold a key while you click, just as you’d hold a button while pressing a pad.

Function keys → right-hand buttons

  • F1↓R1Note
  • F2↓R2Params
  • F3↓R3Prob
  • F4↓R4Pattern
  • F5↓R5Clear
  • F6↓R6Duplicate
  • F7↓R7Record
  • F8↓R8Play

Number row → track buttons

  • 1↓B1Track 1
  • 2↓B2Track 2
  • 3↓B3Track 3
  • 4↓B4Track 4
  • 5↓B5Track 5
  • 6↓B6Track 6
  • 7↓B7Track 7
  • 8↓B8Track 8

Other keys

  • Space↓R8Play, same as F8
  • Shift↓SHIFTLeft or right Shift
  • Esc↓QUITSaves the project

The bar across the top of the window jumps to a mode — PROJECT, SETTINGS, SONG, SCENE, PATTERN, NOTE, SCALE, PARAM, PRESET, PROB — without holding Shift, and the mode you are in is lit. Clicking one presses the buttons it stands for (Shift + F1 for SCALE), so it does exactly what the device does. The hardware has no room for these; only the simulator has them.

Track keys use the top number row, not the keypad. They select tracks on the current track page, so after Shift + 2 the 1 key picks track 9. Everywhere but Global settings and Pattern mode they also go to Note mode; in those two they only move the selection, so you can give one track after another a setting or a pattern without leaving. Shift + 1–8 changes the track page in every mode whose pads work on the selected track, Pattern mode included.

While Shift is held, the simulator relabels the buttons with their Shift functions for the current mode — SCALE, PRESET, SCENE, GLOBAL, PROB and SONG on the right, track pages below — and draws the labels brighter. It also names the top pad row for the pages Shift has turned it into: S1-32–S225-256 in the step modes, so a pad Shift has taken over says what it does rather than what is under it. In Pattern mode Shift takes no pad row, so nothing there is relabelled: every pad is still a pattern.

Where B1–B8 become page buttons they are relabelled too: PG1–PG8 while Shift is held in Project or Preset mode (or R2 in Preset mode), and the steps each page shows (S1-32, S33-64, …) while R1 is held in Note mode on a piano roll track — the one place that gesture still pages.

To help you learn the settings modes, the simulator also prints on the pads what they do: note names, scale names and the keyboard pads in Scale mode, and the settings, +1/−1, channel numbers and the device names in Global settings. The APC mini has no display, so its pads stay plain.

Hold and click

KeysDoes
Hold F5 + clickClears the clicked step, pattern or project. There is no undo. Preset mode has nothing to clear.
Hold F6 + click, clickDuplicates from the first click to each click after it; you can change page in between

Shift + click any other keyPlays that key’s single note on a track whose keyboard is in chordsNote mode

Mouse and faders

ActionDoes
ClickPresses a pad or button for as long as the mouse button is down
Right-clickLatches a pad or button down until you right-click it again. Use it for anything needing several pads at once: latch the keys of a chord, then click a step.
Drag a faderMoves it; clicking anywhere along the slot jumps the cap there
Drag a mixer knobTurns it: dragging up raises it, and about 160 pixels covers the whole range. The knob doesn’t jump when you grab it.
Mouse wheel over a fader or knobNudges it by a thirty-second of its travel
Ctrl + mouse wheelFine steps, a 256th of the travel: about 1 BPM when fader 1 sets the tempo in Global settings

If keys do nothing, click the window once to give it focus. On many laptops F1–F8 change volume or brightness unless you hold Fn or turn on Fn-lock.
