Pattern mode R2

Choose which pattern each track plays. Columns are tracks, rows are patterns, top row first.

  • Tap a pad to make that track play that pattern. It switches immediately, keeping the playhead position.
  • Each column has one green pad: the pattern that track plays. A track whose pattern is on another pattern page shows no green.
  • The bottom row pages the columns, with or without SHIFT: B1–B8 show tracks 1–8, 9–16 and so on to 57–64. Eight pages of eight is every track there is, so the whole row is live. Each button is green for the page on screen, blue for a page holding something, grey for an empty one.
  • SHIFT + the top pad row pages the rows: patterns 1–8, 9–16 and so on to 57–64, one press each. While SHIFT is held that row is the map, in the same colours, so where you are and where you can go are the same eight pads. The pattern page applies to every column.
  • Pages 5–8 are the built-in bank and light amber instead of blue. Tap one and the track plays it at once; R6 copies it down to pages 1–4 to make it yours; R5 leaves it alone.
  • The mixer’s A2 row (REC ARM on a MIDI Mix) mirrors the buttons: A2 1–8 the track pages, lit for the page on screen. It used to be split half tracks and half patterns; the pattern pages have their own row on the pads now, where the page you are on is visible without holding anything.
  • Because the pages are there, SHIFT here means one thing only — the bottom pad row is the mutes — and on the buttons it means the same as in every other mode: SHIFT + R2 opens Scenes, SHIFT + R1 Global settings, and so on. The bottom buttons ignore it, so you can change page with a hand still on the mutes.
  • Tapping a pad also selects its track, which is how you pick one here now that the buttons are pages: touch the column, then R3 to open Note mode on it.
  • SHIFT + a pad on the bottom row mutes that column’s track. While SHIFT is held the bottom row becomes the mutes — the track’s colour means it plays, dark means it is silent — the same way SHIFT turns the buttons into page maps. A muted track’s whole column dims, so it is plain why nothing is coming out of it while you try patterns against a scene.
  • The mutes belong to the session, not to a mode: they stay as they are while you flip between patterns and scenes with SHIFT + R2 and R2, which is what makes auditioning a pattern inside a section work.
  • R5 + pad clears a pattern. R6 + source + destination copies a pattern, also to another track or page.

The built-in bank

Pattern pages 5 to 8 — patterns 33 to 64 — hold material that ships with the instrument instead of material you made. Switch to one of those pages, tap a pad, and the track plays it straight away: there is nothing to load and nothing to set up.

What a track’s bank holds follows its place in its page of eight, so the roles repeat all the way up to track 64.

  • The drum and fill patterns use the General MIDI drum map, and the pitched ones are in C minor, so a drum, a bass and a chord pattern picked at random sit together.
  • Eight of each role’s 32 slots are filled. The rest are empty, left for the set to grow into without moving what is already there.
  • The bank is read-only. R5 will not empty it, the step pads will not edit it, and its patterns keep the length they ship with. To make one yours, hold R6, tap the amber pad, then tap a slot on pages 1–4 — the ordinary pattern copy. Copying into the bank is refused.
  • It costs nothing. The bank is not part of a project, so no project file carries it: an empty project is still 32 bytes with the whole bank available, and clearing a project leaves the bank alone. A track page’s button lights blue for your work only, so the bank does not make every page look busy.
